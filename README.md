## 👋 Your next discovery starts here

A strange file. An unexpected response. A pattern hiding in plain sight.

RUY CTF Academy turns those moments into opportunities to learn. Read a scenario, investigate the evidence, test an idea, and submit your answer in the form of a flag:

```text
RUY{your_discovery_here}
```

That is an example format, not a challenge answer. The fun is finding what belongs inside the braces.

New to CTF? Start with the basics. Already familiar with a topic? Use the exercises to revisit your reasoning and spot gaps in your understanding.

> **Ready to explore?** Open the live academy through this repository’s GitHub Pages deployment. Then choose your first learning track.

## 🧭 Choose your track

**Three tracks. Three ways to investigate.** Expand a track to see what you will practice.

<details>
<summary>🌐 <strong>Web Exploitation — What is the application really doing?</strong></summary>

Explore how applications handle input, access, and data. Work through guided scenarios and local simulations to understand why unexpected behavior happens.

**You will practice:**

- Observing how an application responds to different inputs.
- Recognizing common web security concepts.
- Connecting application behavior to the underlying issue.
- Recording your reasoning instead of relying on guesswork.

**A question to keep asking:** “What did the application assume about this input?”

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔎 <strong>Digital Forensics — What story does the evidence tell?</strong></summary>

Look beyond filenames and first impressions. Examine the supplied evidence, identify useful details, and connect observations into an explanation.

**You will practice:**

- Inspecting metadata and file structures.
- Reading logs and network-related evidence.
- Finding patterns in digital artifacts.
- Reconstructing events from available clues.

**A question to keep asking:** “Which piece of evidence supports this conclusion?”

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔐 <strong>Cryptography — Can you explain the pattern?</strong></summary>

Explore the concepts behind hashes, encryption, signatures, and keys through focused exercises.

**You will encounter:**

- Hashing and XOR.
- RSA and HMAC.
- Digital signatures and trust.
- Key management and implementation mistakes.

**A question to keep asking:** “Which property is this system relying on?”

</details>

## 🎮 Inside the academy

### A path you can follow

Each track moves through **Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced**. Later tiers unlock as you complete the required earlier exercises, giving your practice a clear direction.

### Challenges with feedback

Read the scenario, work through the exercise, and submit your flag. Correct answers count toward your progress and score.

### Hints when you need a nudge

Getting stuck is part of learning. Reveal a hint when you need direction. Each hint reduces the challenge score by 20 points, with a minimum completion score of 20 points.

### Your own investigation notebook

Every challenge includes a place for observations, hypotheses, and things to revisit. Leave yourself an explanation you can understand next week—not just an answer you found today.

### Pick up where you left off

The dashboard shows your progress and offers a way back into an unfinished exercise. Rule-based recommendations help you choose an available challenge to try next.

### Downloadable practice, in two languages

The eight downloadable web playgrounds include **🇮🇩 Indonesian / 🇬🇧 English** controls. Switch the instructions, controls, and result messages while working through the offline HTML exercises.

### Save your discoveries

Progress and notes save automatically in your browser. Export a JSON backup to keep a separate copy, then restore it when moving to another browser or device.

### A milestone at the finish

Complete a learning track to unlock its completion certificate, ready to print or save as a PDF.

## 🎯 Your first mission

Try this small challenge for your first session:

- [ ] Choose one learning track.
- [ ] Read a scenario before trying an answer.
- [ ] Write down one observation and one hypothesis.
- [ ] Complete an exercise, using a hint if needed.
- [ ] Explain why your answer worked in your notes.
- [ ] Export your first progress backup.

**Bonus:** Return to the exercise later. Can you explain your approach without looking at the answer?

*This checklist is a reading guide. Your actual learning progress is tracked inside the academy.*

## 🧠 Build a habit, not just a score

**Observe → Form a hypothesis → Test → Explain → Review**

A flag marks the end of an exercise. Being able to explain the result is what you take into the next one.

Use your notes to record:

- What you noticed first.
- What you expected to happen.
- What the result actually showed.
- What changed your mind.
- What you want to understand better.

## 💬 Before you jump in

<details>
<summary><strong>Do I need to install anything?</strong></summary>

The academy runs in a browser. The core application is contained in a single HTML file. Downloaded HTML playgrounds also run locally in your browser; some evidence exercises can be explored further with your own analysis tools.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Will my progress survive a refresh?</strong></summary>

Yes, when browser storage is available and the save succeeds. Use the same browser profile and website address to return to your saved progress.

Progress is stored locally, not in a cloud account. Clearing site data, ending a private-browsing session, or switching devices can remove access to that local progress. Export a backup regularly.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Is the learning companion an AI agent?</strong></summary>

The current static edition uses rule-based recommendations and a guided learning workflow. It does not include active AI chat or an autonomous agent.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Are the certificates professional certifications?</strong></summary>

No. They recognize completion of a learning track within RUY CTF Academy. They are not accredited professional certifications.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Is this a competitive scoring platform?</strong></summary>

This edition is designed for self-directed practice. Answers and progress are checked in the browser, so it is not a tamper-resistant competition platform. Source obfuscation makes code less readable; it does not keep browser-side answers secret.

</details>
